Research and
Theoretical Frameworks

Overview

This section presents the main theoretical structures developed in the context of mathematical and statistical analysis of financial markets.

The work is based on an independent research approach, aimed at identifying structural properties of market dynamics without reliance on traditional technical indicators or oscillatory tools.

The objective is not predictive optimization, but the formulation of coherent analytical frameworks capable of describing market behavior under reproducible conditions.

Research Direction

The research activity is oriented toward:

The identification of invariant structures in price dynamics
The statistical characterization of non-random components in financial time series
The development of non-optimized analytical models
The formal critique of indicator-based methodologies
The construction of alternative interpretative frameworks grounded in mathematical consistency

Core Principles

All theoretical developments are grounded in the following principles:

— Non-optimization

Models are not derived through parameter fitting or retrospective performance maximization.

— Indicator Independence

No reliance on derived indicators, oscillators, or transformed price signals.

— Structural Analysis

Focus on intrinsic properties of price sequences rather than external overlays.

— Statistical Coherence

All frameworks are required to maintain internal statistical consistency.

— Reproducibility

Theoretical Contributions

Theory I

[Title of the First Theory]

This framework introduces a structural interpretation of price dynamics based on mathematical relations independent of conventional technical constructs. The model focuses on identifying consistent patterns emerging from the internal configuration of price data, without the use of smoothing, filtering, or signal transformation.

Theory II

[Title of the Second Theory]

This work extends the initial framework by incorporating statistical constraints aimed at formalizing the boundaries of interpretability in financial time series. The theory provides a structured critique of commonly used analytical tools and proposes an alternative model grounded in probabilistic consistency.
